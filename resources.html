Construction planning

Building in Uganda while living abroad: a practical preparation checklist

Remote delivery works best when expectations and decisions are agreed before site work begins. Use this checklist to prepare for a useful first project conversation.

Bring these details together

  • Site: location, access, available land documents, and any known site constraints.
  • Brief: intended use, approximate size, priorities, and the people who will approve decisions.
  • Design: drawings or references, preferred materials, finish expectations, and what is still undecided.
  • Delivery: desired start window, update frequency, communication channel, and who can approve variations.
  • Commercial terms: request written scope, inclusions, exclusions, milestones, payment schedule, and change process.

Do not rely on informal updates alone. Agree what evidence is shared at each milestone, how site issues are escalated, and who records decisions. Approvals, insurance, warranties, and professional responsibilities should be confirmed for the specific project in writing.

Budget and estimates

What to prepare before requesting a construction estimate

A useful estimate depends on a clear scope. The more relevant project information you can provide, the easier it is to identify assumptions and questions that need resolution.

Include, where available

  • Project location and a short description of the proposed work.
  • Current drawings, measurements, specifications, or a reference design.
  • Preferred structural and finishing materials, or options that need pricing.
  • Known site conditions, access limitations, existing structures, and utilities.
  • Target timing and any required approvals or dependencies.

Ask the estimator to distinguish measured quantities from allowances, and to state exclusions, taxes, assumptions, and validity period. A preliminary online block estimate is only an indicative quantity calculation—not a complete bill of quantities, project budget, or quotation.

Property management

Managing property from a distance: define the operating rules first

Before appointing anyone to coordinate property work, document the authority, reporting, and expense rules that protect the owner and make decisions easier.

Agree these points in writing

  • Which inspections, routine checks, and maintenance tasks are included.
  • Who may contact vendors, approve work, or authorize spending—and up to what limit.
  • How urgent issues are handled and which contacts can make time-sensitive decisions.
  • What updates, receipts, photographs, and records are shared, and how often.
  • How fees, vendor costs, access, keys, and service changes are managed.

Keep ownership documents and key decisions in records you control. Confirm local legal, tax, and regulatory questions with appropriately qualified advisers; service arrangements and responsibilities depend on the property and agreement.
